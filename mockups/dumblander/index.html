Dumb Lander // 240x240 mockup

The dumb_lander.p8 cart moved off PICO-8's 128x128 at 30 Hz and onto the PicoSystem's 240x240 at 100 Hz. The flight model is the cart's, converted rather than retuned: gravity, thrust, the sideways nudge and the 1.0 crash threshold all still come from the same four numbers. What is new is the shape around them, because the cart was one landing and a key that reset it. Nothing here is built yet. This page exists so the feel can be flown and argued about before anyone writes C++, which is CLAUDE.md rule 10.

PLAY IT

Take off from the grey pad, put it down on the gold one. One press of the main engine is worth two of gravity, so a tap is a hop and a hold is a climb. The sideways jets are free, the main engine is not.

WHAT IS ON SCREEN, AND WHY

  • Fuel bar, top left. The cart had this and the cart was right. It goes amber at a third and red at an eighth, so the moment to stop being ambitious arrives before the tank is empty rather than after.
  • SPD, under the bar. Pixels per second, green while a touchdown would survive and red while it would not. This is the whole game read as one number, so it is the one number that gets a colour.
  • LEG, top right. How many pads you have crossed this run. The score, and the only reason to keep flying.
  • Nothing names a button. Rule 9. The cart printed "X to reset" in the corner of every frame, which is exactly what the rule forbids: any button restarts, so no press can be the wrong guess and the line disappears. The gallery card lists the controls for anyone who wants them.
  • No arrow to the goal. The world is exactly one screen wide, the same as the cart. The gold pad is always visible, so pointing at it would be a HUD element earning nothing. Worth stating so nobody adds a camera this game does not need.

WHAT 240x240 CHANGES

  • The lander stops being a rumour. The cart drew an 8x8 sprite: four pixels of hull and two legs. At 240 it is 15 wide with a canopy, splayed legs and an engine bell, and the flame is three bands instead of five random pixels.
  • Sub pixel motion reads. At 128 with a 30 Hz tick, a lander drifting at the crash threshold moved a whole pixel per frame. At 240 with a 100 Hz tick the same physical speed is 0.56 px per tick, so a slow approach looks slow instead of looking like a slideshow.
  • The terrain gets its detail back. Same two cosine bands as the cart, sampled over 240 columns instead of 120, with a lit rim and a shaded depth the 128 version had no pixels for.
  • The HUD stops overlapping the world. The cart's speed readout sat at y=32, in the sky the lander flies through. There is room for it now.

WHAT THE CART DID, AND WHAT THIS DOES INSTEAD

  • A run, not a screen. The cart ended at the first landing and gave you a reset key. Here a clean landing is a leg: the world regenerates, the gold pad moves further out and narrows by 2 px, the tank tops up by 55, and the leg count goes up. A wreck ends the run. This is the one change that turns the cart into something a gallery card can promise, and it is also the one most worth vetoing: say the word and it goes back to a single pad with a restart.
  • Landing on the pad means being on the pad. The cart tested x >= goal.x and x + 8 <= goal.x + 16, where x is the hull's centre, so it actually demanded the centre sit in the left half of the pad and the right half of the pad did not count. Here the whole 15 px footprint has to be inside the pad. Same difficulty, no dead half.
  • Rocks hold still. The cart picked each rock's colour with rnd inside _draw, so all thirty strobed every frame. Colour is chosen once when the rock is placed.
  • Being out of fuel on the wrong ground is an ending. The cart let you settle gently anywhere and then sit there with an empty tank forever. A soft touchdown off the pad is still just a rest, and you can take off again, but resting with nothing left in the tank ends the run as STRANDED rather than deadlocking.
  • A ceiling. The cart let the lander leave the top of the screen and come back. Here it stops at y=8 with its climb cancelled, because a lander you cannot see is a lander you cannot fly.
  • Kept exactly: everything else. Gravity, thrust, the sideways impulse, the 1.0 crash threshold, the tank of 100 draining one unit per thrusting frame, the two cosine terrain bands and their clamp, the pad flattening, the take-off latch that stops the opening frame counting as a crash. All of it converted by the table below, none of it retuned.

THE CONVERSION, NUMBER BY NUMBER

The cart runs _update, which is 30 Hz, not _update60. The device runs the SDK's update(), which is one tick per 10 ms. So a per frame velocity is multiplied by 1.875 * (30/100), a per frame acceleration by 1.875 * (30/100)^2, and a count of frames by 100/30.

WHAT IT COSTS THE DEVICE

  • Framebuffer 115 KB of the 264 KB, because 240x240 at 16 bits is what ScreenMode::hires costs. chicken already pays it.
  • Terrain 480 B. 240 columns of uint16_t, in Q8.8 so the ridge is smooth rather than staircased.
  • Rocks 336 B. 56 rocks of 6 bytes: x, y, radius, colour, shape.
  • Stars 180 B. 60 stars of 3 bytes.
  • Flame particles 288 B. 24 of 12 bytes, a fixed array with an active flag, never allocated.
  • Everything else is the lander itself, which is six Q16.16 scalars, so 24 bytes.
  • Total on top of the framebuffer: about 1.3 KB. There is no pressure here at all. Flash is a few KB of code and no baked assets, since the terrain is generated and rule 11 does not apply to a game with no models.
