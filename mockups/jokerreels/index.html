Joker Reels // 240x240 mockup

A 3D slot machine with a Balatro shaped run around it. Three drums you can actually see turning, a speed dial that trades reading the symbols against a multiplier, jokers that fire while the score counts, hands you level up, and the ability to open a drum and change what it is able to land on. Nothing is built yet: this page exists so the design can be played and argued about before anyone writes C++, which is CLAUDE.md rule 10.

PLAY IT

Set the speed, pull, and stop the drums yourself if you dare. A slow drum can be read and aimed at. A fast one cannot, and pays a multiplier for the privilege. Every reel you leave to stop on its own pays nothing and risks nothing.

SCREENS

THE IDEA

  • A slot machine is a bad game and a great verb. Pulling is satisfying and deciding nothing is not, so everything here is about giving the pull a decision attached to it. There are three: what speed to spin at, when to stop each drum, and what the drums are able to land on at all.
  • The speed dial is the risk. SLOW draws the symbol on the front face clearly, so you can watch one come round and stop it. FAIR greys it, so you can time a stop but not read it comfortably. WILD does not draw it at all and is worth +5 mult a reel. That is the whole gamble, and it is one button.
  • Stopping is optional. Leave every drum to stop itself and you score nothing extra and take no risk, which is the floor. The BLUR joker pays x2 for doing exactly that, which is the kind of inversion Balatro builds its best runs out of.
  • Chips and mult are separate and both large. The tally plays back one line at a time: the hand, then each symbol, then each stop bonus, then each joker in order. Watching one number climb is the feel being borrowed, and a single total would throw it away.
  • Opening a drum is the deckbuilding. Each drum is a ring of eight faces, and buying a swap lets you replace one. Put a CROWN on and that drum can land on a crown for ever; the symbol you replaced is gone from it. That is the run's long arc, and it is the one screen where the 3D is doing work a 2D layout could not.

WHAT IS ON SCREEN, AND WHY

  • The top 112 rows are the machine, in 3D. Everything below is an ordinary 2D panel. See the note on the window below: this split is what makes a hires 3D game affordable at all.
  • Ante, bank against target, spins, gold across one strip. Four numbers, which is what a run needs and no more.
  • The score box is chips and mult, not a total. The total is one small line under it, because it is the outcome and they are the decision.
  • One tally line at a time. The count is the animation.
  • The joker row is five slots and shows what you have, not what they do. What a joker does is on the shop card when you buy it and in the tally when it fires, which is the two moments you need it.
  • Nothing names a button. Rule 9.

THE 3D WINDOW, AND WHY IT IS 112 ROWS

Every 3D game in this repo runs at lores 120x120, and engine/include/pse/config.hpp says why in as many words: the depth buffer is width * height bytes, 120x120 costs 14,400, and going to 240x240 would be 57.6 KB on top of a 115 KB hires framebuffer. A Balatro-shaped UI does not fit in 120x120, so that would normally be the end of the idea.

It is not, because of something the engine already does. pse::Rasterizer indexes its depth buffer as y * target.width + x on whatever RenderTarget it is handed, and RenderTarget carries row_stride separately from width precisely so its rows need not be packed. So a target pointing at row 0 of a 240x240 screen, with height 112 and the screen's own stride, gives a 3D window in the top band and a depth buffer that only covers that band. PSE_RENDER_WIDTH and PSE_RENDER_HEIGHT are already per game overridable through DEFINES.

Whether that is affordable in time on a 250 MHz M0+ is not something this page can answer and it should not pretend to: config.hpp is blunt that nobody has measured the time budget on hardware. What it can say is the triangle count and the fill, which are measured below and are small, because the scene is one machine and never grows.

DOES THE TEXT FIT

WHAT IT COSTS

  • The scene is 3 drums of 8 faces, 2 end fans each, and 3 cabinet quads. 27 quads of 2 triangles is 54 triangles plus 48 fan triangles, against a queue that holds 640. There is no world to stream and no LOD to pick.
  • The symbol is a screen space sprite, not a texture. The engine has perspective correct texturing and it would work, but only the face at the front of a drum is ever read, and at the front it is nearly flat on to the camera. One 5x5 pip drawn at the projected centre of the front face, squashed by how far that face has turned, reads the same and costs nothing per pixel.
  • Game state is tiny: three rings of eight bytes, five joker indices, four hand levels, and a tally of at most a dozen entries. Under 200 bytes.
  • Art is the eight symbol pips and whatever the cabinet needs, which is the one thing this page has not designed and would want real PNGs for (rule 11 as it now applies to sprites).

OPEN QUESTIONS

  • Is the 112 row window the right split? It is the number that makes the panel hold a score box, a speed dial, a tally line and five joker slots without crowding. More rows to the machine means a cramped panel; fewer means the drums stop reading as objects.
  • Three drums or five? Three keeps a hand readable at a glance and the drums big. Five gives room for real poker hands and makes each drum small and each swap cheaper. This page is built on three.
  • Should stopping a drum be free? Right now leaving them all to stop themselves is the safe floor and one joker pays for it. The alternative is that not stopping costs you, which would force engagement and remove the floor.
  • Eight jokers is a demo, not a game. Balatro ships 150. What makes this worth building is whether twenty or so can be written that interact, and that is a design job to do before the C++, not after.
