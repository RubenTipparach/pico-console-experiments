Cat Coin Pusher // 240x240 mockup

The cat_coin_pusher.p8 cart moved off PICO-8's 128x128 at 60 Hz and onto the PicoSystem's 240x240 at 100 Hz. All of it comes across: eleven special coins with their two second fuses, the combo spinner, the shop, ten rounds and a target that grows by half each time. What changes is that the table is 4.9 times the area and the panel under it is no longer four pixel text in a 50 pixel strip. Nothing is built yet. This page exists so the layout and the feel can be argued about before anyone writes C++, which is CLAUDE.md rule 10.

PLAY IT

Drop coins on the pile until enough of it falls off the front edge. The pusher does the work, the combo does the scoring, and gold between rounds buys the coins that break a stuck table open.

WHAT IS ON SCREEN, AND WHY

  • Top strip: round, buffs, coins, gold. Four things, each one a letter and a number. The buffs only appear while they are running.
  • The progress bar carries the score, and the score appears nowhere else. The cart printed it twice, in the top strip and again on the bar, because at 128 neither copy was quite readable. One readable copy is better than two cramped ones.
  • The combo meter sits under the lip, which is where the combo happens. It fills toward the five that opens the spinner, so the thing you are chasing is drawn at the place you are watching.
  • One selectable row, and it names no buttons. This is the rule 9 problem this cart poses, and it is the most interesting design call on the page. See below.
  • A fuse is a ring and some dots, not a number. The ring tightens as the two seconds burn down and there is one dot per second left.

THE ROW THAT REPLACED THE BUTTON PROMPTS

The cart's bottom panel was three button prompts: "⬇:buy 5 coins g5", "end round❎" and "🅰❎ to start" on the title. CLAUDE.md rule 9 forbids naming a button on screen, so those three lines cannot come across as they are. Selection carries the meaning instead: the five bag slots, a BUY chip and an END ROUND chip are all slots on one row, left and right move along it, and one button uses whatever is selected. A line under the row says what the selection does, in words about the game rather than words about the controller. Dropping a coin is the verb of the game and keeps a button of its own. Nothing on the screen names a key, and the gallery card lists them for anyone who wants them.

WHAT 240x240 CHANGES

  • The table is 4.9 times the area, 208 x 112 against the cart's 108 x 44, and it is proportionally taller as well as wider. A coin pusher wants depth to pile into and the vertical is what 240x240 hands over.
  • A coin is 10 px across instead of 5, which is the difference between a special being a colour and a special being a face. Eleven of the cart's specials had to be told apart by hue alone at r 2.5.
  • The cat is a cat. At 128 it was one circfill and four lines. It is the game's whole personality, so it gets the pixels: ears that twitch, eyes that blink, whiskers, and paws holding the coin it is about to drop.
  • The shop can say what an item does on a line of its own instead of sharing sixteen pixels of height with its own name and price.
  • The bottom panel stops being a crush. The cart fitted a progress bar, five inventory slots, a description line, an end button and a buy line into y 76 to 127. The same five things now have 84 px.

WHAT THE CART DID, AND WHAT THIS DOES INSTEAD

  • No button named on any screen. Rule 9, as above.
  • The field is proportionally taller, 1.86:1 rather than the cart's 2.45:1. This is a real change to how the game plays: a coin has a longer run from the pusher to the lip, so the pile is deeper and a shove travels further. The pusher plate and its sweep were scaled by the field height rather than by 1.875 so that they keep the same share of the table, and its speed was then set so a sweep still takes the cart's 0.76 seconds. If you would rather keep the cart's exact proportions, the field becomes 208 x 85 and 27 px of screen goes back to the panel.
  • The score is drawn once. As above.
  • The felt speckle holds still. The cart re-scattered its 50 felt pixels every frame from rnd inside _draw, so the table fizzed. Same pattern, computed from the index.
  • A special going off flashes the cabinet edge for six ticks rather than doing nothing visible. With 200 loose coins on the table, a bomb needs to announce itself.
  • Kept exactly: all eleven specials and their radii, impulses and durations; the two second fuse and the fact that buying coins sets every fuse off early; the goldfish being passive and worth 50; the combo multiplier min(combo,8) * comboMult; the spinner at a combo of five with a multiplier of how many times over; the shop's three offers and its refresh at 1.5x; targets of 150 * 1.5^(round-1); ten rounds; gold paid out per ceil(target/100) of score.

THE CONVERSION, NUMBER BY NUMBER

The cart runs _update60, so 60 Hz, not the 30 Hz of the lander cart. A per frame velocity is multiplied by 1.875 * (60/100), an acceleration by 1.875 * (60/100)^2, a count of frames by 100/60, and a per frame damping factor d becomes d^0.6, because damping compounds: 0.92 sixty times a second is 0.9509 a hundred times a second, not 0.92.

WHAT IT COSTS THE DEVICE

  • Framebuffer 115 KB of the 264 KB, for 240x240 at 16 bits.
  • Coins 6.8 KB. 340 slots of 20 bytes: x, y, vx, vy in Q16.16, plus a type, a flag byte and a fuse. 340 rather than the 243 a round 10 table seeds, because the clone special adds five at a time.
  • Collision grid 1.4 KB. A head index per cell over an 18 x 11 grid plus a next index per coin, both int16_t. No allocation and no hash map: the grid is a fixed array cleared each tick.
  • Particles, popups and payout coins 2.2 KB. 96 particles, 16 popups and 32 falling coins, all fixed arrays with an active flag, which is rule 8's shape rather than a std::vector.
  • Everything else is scalars, under 200 bytes: the pusher, the dispenser, the bag of five, the shop of three, the spinner of three.
  • Total about 10.6 KB on top of the framebuffer, so roughly 126 KB of 264 KB. Comfortable. Flash is code plus the thumbnail; there are no models, so rule 11 does not apply.
